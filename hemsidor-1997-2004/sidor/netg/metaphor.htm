Summary of: Working with Interface Metaphors

Article in Human Computer Interaction: Toward the Year 2000
By Thomas D. Erickson, former Apple Research Laboratories, now The Pliant Research Group a group focused on the mismatch between the rigidity of current computing technology and the richness of human lives and practices.

Defintions

A metaphor is an invisible web of terms and associations that underlies the way we speak and think about a concept.

Sense 1: metaphor, implicit comparison
imaginative comparison

Sense 2: metaphor -- (a figure of speech in which an expression is used to refer to something that it does not literally denote in order to suggest a similarity)
trope, figure of speech, figure, image -- (language used in a figurative or nonliteral sense)

[Source: Advanced, WordNet Vocabulary Helper via OneLook Dictionaries]

The transference of the relation between one set of objects to another set for the purpose of brief explanation; a compressed simile; e. g., the ship plows the sea. Abbott & Seeley. All the world's a stage." Shak. &hand; The statement, that man is a fox," is a metaphor; but that man is like a fox," is a simile, similitude, or comparison.

Metaphors and Human Computer Interface

The process of generating interface metaphors:

1. Functional Definition

Understand how the thing itself works.

(Example: Automated copy and paste - one way, one to many, may be a time lag - described as define links)

2. Identify User's Problems

No metaphor can model all aspects of a system's functionality, the designer must identify what parts of it are most likely to give users difficulty.

3. Metaphor Generation

Don't just use the abstract concept from the description of the functionality. Look for real-word events, objects, or institutions that embody some of the characteristics that users find difficult to understand.

(Used abstract concept: links between, from source to destination, data sent along the links. - Compare to paths, wires, pipes all special case of the links metaphor = Connection metaphor. Proposed metaphors links, TV broadcasting, and newspaper publishing)

4. Evaluating Metaphors

Amount of Structure - A metaphor without structure may not be useful!

(TV broadcasting, newspaper publishing better than links)

Applicability of Structure - How much of the metaphor are relevant to the problem? "False-friend?"

(Newspaper publishing better than TV broadcasting)

Representability - Is the interface metaphor easy to represent? Distinct visual and auditory representation? Specific words to associated with them?

(TV broadcasting, newspaper publishing better than links)

Suitability to Audience - Do users grasp the metaphor?

Extensibility - May have additional bits of structure that may be useful later on?

(A server on a network having a newsstand, recipient of publications)

An interface metaphor provides users with realistic expectations about what will happen it enhances the utility of the system.

In Swedish

Metaforer används för att användaren skall kunna relatera till saker ur verkligheten och tillämpa kunskaper från ett område till ett annat. Detta för att förenkla användandet. Man överför alltså sin kunskap från en ursprungsdomän till en tillämpningsdomän.

Man skiljer i samband med gränssnittsdesign på två typer av metoforer

  • konversationsmetafor där systemet fungerar som konversationspartner
  • modelvärlds-metafor där användaren direktmanipulerar objekt i tillämpningen
