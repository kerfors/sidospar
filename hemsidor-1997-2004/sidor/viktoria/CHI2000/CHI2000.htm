This is a short report from the CHI2000 conference in Hague the first week in April 2000, written by Kerstin Forsberg.

What's CHI

In USA the area is called Computer-Human Interaction, in Europe the acronym HCI (Human-Computer Interaction) is more often used.

The Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI), is the leading international forum for the exchange of ideas and information about human-computer interaction (HCI). From Viktoria we were about 20 people visiting CHI2000. Totally, more than 250 Swedish researchers and practitioners from companies like Framfab, Icon, VM-data as well as Telia and Ericsson participated in this highly recommended conference.

The CHI-conference is arranged by ACM SIGCHI. ACM (Association for Computing Machinery) is the world's first educational and scientific computing society, with over 80,000 members. SIG stands for Special Interest group. The SIGCHI serves as an international venue for specialists in human-computer interaction, education, usability, interaction design, computer supported cooperative work, and other related areas. A newly organised local SIGCHI is the West Sweden CHI.

More than 250 people participated in a tutorial about "Designing Usable Mobile Services". The number was at least 100 more than the organizer and the instructors expected - an indicator of the enormous interest in this area. Two experienced mobile services developers from Nokia and Motorola didn't reach the goal of the workshop. The tutorial included a eight step model of a design process. The proposed model was critized since it was a very general design model and didn't adress the specifics concerning mobile services and the work during the workshop was to fragmented to add to the model. However, I think it could be used to define a similar Mobile Business Service Development Model, and also to use it as a container and indexer of best-practices, lessons learned, checklists etc.

Examples of mobile phones that has T9 Text Input are Ericsson R280d, Nokia 7110 and 8210

On the conference a few paper addressed mobile interfaces. From Nokia a paper describing and evaluating the so called T9-technique. With T9 a mobile phone user can double the text entry rate from 25-27 wpm (words per minute), with a traditional multi-press method on the 12-key keypad, to 41-46 wpm. In Finland, as well as in Sweden, this is highly relevant since more then 650 millions of SMS was send during 1999. T9 Text Input gives the phone's standard keypad the capabilities of a full ' keyboard. By using the letters grouped on each of the phone keys and a highly compressed database, T9 software requires just one key press per letter, and quickly recognizes the most commonly used word of a numeric sequence.

What is Context-awareness?

One of the most obvious mobile applications that seem to be one of the first can imagine is a tourist guide that is context-aware. That is an application that is aware of the tourist's location and present information tailored to that environment e.g. presenting information about the castle that the user is outside.

The context-aware discussion was also the issue for a workshop called "The What, Who, Where, When, Why and How of Context-Awareness" Context has been defined as "any information that can be used to characterise the situation of an entity, where an entity can be a person, place, physical or computational object”. And context-aware as "the use of context to provide task-relevant information and/or services to a user, wherever they may be".

The Guide application itself wasn't especially interesting and the authors just mentioned the interesting consequence of such an application on the local business model there local business consider the application an important avenue for marketing their products.

E-business

You could test the SmartClient Travel Guided Demo in Java

Surprisingly, few papers discussed e-business, e-commerce, interfaces and related questions. One exception was an interesting paper describing SmartClient that is a new technique for adapting the interaction with en electronic catalogue to the actual buying behaviour.

There are 6 stages of consumer buying behaviour: need identification, product brokering, merchant brokering, negotiating and purchase and delivery. (From an nice article: 'Agents that can buy and sell' in Communications in the ACM, March 1999 by Pattie Maes from MIT, working with Software Agents and the Future of Electronic Commerce.

In this paper the authors argues that, in opposite to must e-commerce user interaction, needs identification, product brokering and comparision should be an iterative conversation where criteria and proposals are being exchanged. SmartClient uses CSP-techniques (Constraint Satisfaction Problem) to generate a solution space, letting the user navigate in this and narrow down the choices.

Such an approach could be useful when adding e-services to an electronic catalogues.

Styling the Web

Several tutorials had web-usability as it common theme. In a report from Forrester Usability was the second reason why persons returned to a site. 6 6 % did find usability important. The first criteria was, of course; Good content (75%) and number three was speed (58 %) and frequent update (54 %).

UWISH - Advocating the "design for all" approach, specific attention is paid to the accessibility for elderly.

One example of usability is the measured factors that affected web-navigation for elderly users: spatial ability for mental modelling, memory capacity for task switching, and situation awareness during interaction. To help elderly, these three factors were used to design three navigation functions: categorization landmarks, history maps and a navigation assistant. This is part of the work done by UWISH, Usability of Web-based Information Services for Hypermedia.

As you can see from this report I did attend a tutorial in CSS (Cascading Style Sheet) - Styling the New Web: Usability with CSS. Pretty basic stuff - but interesting to see the possibilities of separating style and content. The main news in the CSS3 (under development) seems to be better control of printing, multicolumn, headers and footers.

You can test out Cha-Cha - A System for Organizing Intranet Search Results

An interesting paper presented at the conference discussed how to Bring Order to the Web: Automatically Categorizing Search Result. People from University of California and Microsoft have developed Cha-Cha, a System for Organizing Intranet Search Results, which uses this approach to organise search results.

People from Pal Alto Laboratory presented a method for generating compact pictorial summarizations of video called Comic Book . Images are laid out in a compact, visually pleasing display reminiscent of a comic book or Japanese manga. Users can explore the video by interacting with the presented summary. This seems to be a very nice way of exploring e.g. educational material.
