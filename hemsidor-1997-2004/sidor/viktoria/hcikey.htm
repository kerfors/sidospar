Human-Computer Interaction:
Key words and theories

Updated by Kerstin Forsberg 1 May 1997

This is a summary, done by Kerstin Forsberg, as part of my Human-Computer Interaction - HCI course at the Viktoria Institute for Research on Information Technology Use. See more about this on my HCI-page.

Affordances | Chunks | Focus+Context (or Fisheye) | Gestalt Theory | Metaphor | Perception |

Affordances

An affordance describes the relationship between the person and the environment. A table affords support such as sitting, placing papers on it, placing food dishes on it, and standing on it. It also affords throwing (if it is light enough) and hiding behind.

Technological affordances means that some technologies afford two-way communication, some do not. Some afford reflection, some do not. Some afford efficient communication or transmission of knowledge, some do not. Some afford exploratory learning, some do not.

  • The idea of affordances, from James J. Gibson, see: Information Pickup Theory
  • The idea of technological affordances from The design of everyday things by Donald Norman

Chunks

Miller (1956) presented the idea that short-term memory could only hold 5-9 chunks of information (seven plus or minus two) where a chunk is any meaningful unit. A chunk could refer to digits, words, chess positions, or people's faces. The concept of chunking and the limited capacity of short term memory became a basic element of all subsequent theories of memory.
  • The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two: Some Limits on Our Capacity for Processing Information
  • George A. Miller
  • Information Processing Theory (G. Miller)

Focus+Context (or Fisheye)

Focus+Context (or Fisheye) techniques, in which detailed views of particular parts of an information set are blended in some way with a view of the overall structure of the set and operations for dynamic manipulation are provided.

Nonlinear magnification is a term that encompasses many related techniques which have been described in the literature under such names as 'fisheye views' 'distortion-based viewing' and 'stretching the rubber sheet'.

  • See the description of Hyperbolic Tree in the Summary of the article: Rich Interaction in the Digital Library

Gestalt Theory

Gestalt theory is a broadly interdisciplinary general theory which provides a framework for a wide variety of psychological phenomena, processes, and applications. Human beings are viewed as open systems in active interaction with their environment.
  • Society for Gestalt Theory and its Applications
  • Gestalt Theory (Wertheimer)

Metaphor

A metaphor is an invisible web of terms and associations that underlies the way we speak and think about a concept. An interface metaphor provides users with realistic expectations about what will happen it enhances the utility of the system.
  • Summary of the article: Working with Interface Metaphors

Theories of Perception

The perceptual principles that allow people to group visually similar objects into entities, or groups, have been referred. One of the most important theories of perception is the Gestalt Theory.
